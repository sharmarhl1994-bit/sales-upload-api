Growth Cap: ±80% → ±40%
If a store grew 60% last year, the old model assumed it would grow 42% again — too optimistic. Capping growth at ±40% prevents over-extrapolation from exceptional years.

Also added: Inactive/Closed stores excluded from forecast pool. 24 stores that were underperforming were removed.
Before: −23%
After: −1.7%
2
Grade Calibration Factors
Not all grades behave the same. We compared actual vs forecast for each grade using real H1 data and derived correction multipliers:

A++ × 1.20  |  C+ × 0.89  |  FO/C × 0.65  |  A+B/C+ × 0.87

Now every grade is within ±7% at category level.
C+: −11%
C+: +0.1%
3
New Store Peer-Based Forecasting
New stores have no 2-year history, so the old model forecast them at near-zero. The actual was 531% higher.

New logic: Match with 3+ mature stores of same grade + zone, compute their average seasonal pattern and annual run-rate, then apply a 60% Year-1 ramp factor (derived from actual H1: new stores do 58% of what mature peers do in Year 1).
Before: +544%
After: ~−5%

Festival Calendar Integration
A DB table with 2024–2027 Hindu, Islamic, national and western festivals with impact scores (1–10). When Diwali shifts from October to November (like in 2026), the model automatically adjusts November forecast up and October down — up to ±15% per month.

Activates after you run sql/festivals.sql in pgAdmin.

Why portfolio is accurate but individual stores vary: Errors cancel each other out at portfolio level. Store A at +15% and Store B at −12% = net +1.5% portfolio. This is normal in all retail forecasting — even tier-1 retail companies target portfolio-level accuracy, not individual store precision.
